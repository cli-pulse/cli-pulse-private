# v1.61.0 release notes

**Marketing version**: 1.61.0
**Apple CFBundleVersion**: 116
**Android versionCode**: 82 (source only: no Android build is made or
published with this release)
**Platforms**: the Mac (Mac App Store, and Developer ID / Homebrew), iPhone
and iPad, and Apple Watch.
**Backend**: no migration, no edge-function deploy. Nothing under `backend/`
changed since 1.60.1.
**Companion CLI**: unchanged at 1.33.0. `helper/` has not changed, so no
Companion `.pkg` ships with this release.

Theme: **the Watch gets its sign-in from the iPhone, and sign-in recovers on
its own**.

---

## B1: the Watch asks the iPhone for a token (#732, #738, #739, #740; iPhone + Watch)

Supabase refresh tokens are single-use, and the iPhone used to hand the Watch
both its access and its refresh token, after which both refreshed the same
chain. Now there is one refresher per session:

- A Watch signed in through the iPhone holds the access token only and asks
  the iPhone for a new one (`WatchCredentialRelay`). The iPhone forwards every
  rotation and answers with its access token only; it never sends the refresh
  token. A Watch signed in with a code on the Watch keeps renewing itself.
- The iPhone, woken by the Watch while locked, keeps its session: a rotation
  the locked Keychain refuses is kept in memory and written again on unlock
  (`SessionTokenStore`), no refresh runs while it could not be written, and a
  locked Keychain is no longer read as "never signed in" (#738).
- The Watch keeps a token delivered while its restore is still running, sends
  a fingerprint of the token it holds, and cancels stale queued questions
  (#739).
- While it waits for a locked or distant iPhone, the Watch stays signed in,
  stops resending a refused token (a 5 to 30 minute hold), and its error card
  says "Data will update once your iPhone is unlocked and nearby." A Watch
  upgraded from a build before 1.61 keeps its session (#740).

The real-device test (locked iPhone with its app killed, an expired token)
passed on 04edcdd1; #740 was not device-tested.

## B4: the Watch's Live page and Devices chip (#733; Watch)

The Live page lists sessions by freshness on the iPhone's rule and clock, so
a "Running" row whose device stopped syncing is gone, and the Devices chip is
counted on the Watch from a device-presence list (seen in the last 30
minutes), with the server's figure only as a fallback.

## B2: the refresh a session restore skipped runs when it ends (#729; Mac, iPhone)

The refresh at launch was skipped while the restore held `isLoading`, so the
first upload from a Mac whose menu was not opened waited for the next timer
tick, up to 30 minutes. A skipped refresh is now owed and runs once when the
flag clears; a sign-out drops it.

## B3: a deferred restore retries on activation and network return (#730; Mac, iPhone)

While a restore is deferred (offline start, #725), it also tries at once when
the app becomes active (Mac: activation or the menu opening; iPhone/iPad: the
scene becoming active) or the network goes from unsatisfied to satisfied. One
try at a time, at least 5 s apart; the backoff is unchanged.

## #735: a sign-in or sign-out during a restore wins (all Apple platforms)

A restore answers only for the session generation it started in, and a
sign-out fences the session in flight, so a restore or refresh still out when
the user signs in or out can no longer sign back in or leave tokens behind.

## B5: "Hide personal information" numbers this Mac's own rows (#731; Mac)

This Mac's own session rows, named by its host name, now get the number of
its paired device ("Device N") instead of "Hidden device". Nothing changes on
the iPhone, iPad or Watch.

## P2, P3: Copilot (#734; Mac, and wherever provider cards show)

- An account whose subscription has ended reads "Copilot subscription ended",
  not "Operational".
- The monthly Premium and chat quotas carry their window (the calendar month
  ending at the reset, in UTC), so Copilot gets a pace line and marker.
- The `premium_interactions` snapshot is read, so the Premium bar appears.
